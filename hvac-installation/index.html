Where do most HVAC installations go wrong? Most often before the first wrench turns, when someone matches the new tonnage to the old nameplate and skips the math. At Medford HVAC, the math comes first. We build a load calculation for your Medford, OR home from its square footage, insulation levels, windows, orientation, and air leakage, because a system sized by habit rather than by measurement is wrong from day one. From there the job becomes three connected pieces of work: equipment matched to the load, ductwork able to deliver what that equipment produces, and a commissioning process that proves the result with real readings instead of on paper.

Equipment selection follows the load, and matching matters more than most homeowners are told. The indoor coil, outdoor unit, and blower are rated as matched combinations, and mismatched pairings give up the SEER2 and AFUE numbers on the sticker. We walk you through the real trade-offs between single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed equipment, blower options, and pairings with a furnace, air handler, or heat pump, in plain language and without pressure. Then the ductwork gets the attention it almost never receives: our experienced technicians measure static pressure, hunt down leaky and undersized runs, and correct the chokepoints while the system is open, because a duct system that fights the equipment defeats it.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a fast one part ways. Our technicians pressure-test and evacuate the line set to a micron-verified vacuum, weigh in the charge and prove it with superheat and subcooling readings, leak-test the gas side, vent to the manufacturer's spec, and trap the condensate with float-switch protection. The thermostat gets configured for your equipment's real staging rather than left on defaults. Then comes full commissioning: temperature split, temperature rise, amp draws, and static pressure, all recorded and explained to you before we haul the old system away. Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Installation?

Most installation mistakes stay hidden on the day the system starts. Skip the vacuum verification and moisture stays in the refrigerant loop, where it slowly forms acid. Set the charge by feel and efficiency drains away without anyone noticing. Pair a coil and condenser that were never rated together and the sticker numbers become impossible to reach. Leave the duct restrictions in place and the blower spends the next fifteen years straining against static pressure it was not built for. On top of that, refrigerant handling is federally regulated, and a gas or venting error is a safety issue, not just a comfort one.

Our experienced technicians treat installation as a chain of verifications, not a list of tasks. The load calculation proves the size. Matched equipment makes the rating reachable. A pressure test and a micron-gauge vacuum prove the circuit is tight and dry, while a weighed charge checked against superheat and subcooling proves the refrigerant side. On the heating side, gas lines are leak-tested, draft is verified, and blower speed is set to hold the rated temperature rise. Finally, commissioning puts the numbers on paper, so every future service visit has something to check against. That documentation is standard on every system we install in Medford.

How to Prevent HVAC Installation Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Save your commissioning report and share it with whoever services the system
  • Change filters on schedule from day one; airflow protects both the coil and the exchanger
  • Flush the drain line before summer to keep algae from clogging it
  • Leave supply and return vents open so the system stays balanced the way it was commissioned
  • Book professional maintenance twice a year, spring for cooling and fall for heating
  • Call about new noises or slipping performance early, when fixes are simple
  • Give the outdoor unit clear space on every side and a gentle rinse each season
